## STEP 1: WRITING STYLE GUIDE

### Sentence Structure
- Default: Short-to-medium punchy sentences. Leads with the point, not the reasoning.
- When explaining technical things or defending a position: shifts to long, dense, single-paragraph blocks with multiple clauses chained by commas and "and." No paragraph breaks mid-thought.
- Rarely uses transitions or filler sentences. Jumps straight from point to point.
- Lists things with commas in running text rather than formal bullet points, unless the context is instructional.

### Punctuation
- **Periods:** Skips them in single-sentence texts. Uses them when writing multiple sentences.
- **Exclamation marks:** Uses freely when excited or being playful. "LETS GO!" "No Fucking Way!" "I got it!"
- **Question marks:** Normal usage.
- **Ellipses:** Avoids entirely. Considers them confusing.
- **Dashes:** Uses for visual separation and mid-sentence pivots.
- **Parentheses:** Frequent. Used to add context without cluttering the main sentence.
- **ALL CAPS:** Used for emphasis on key words, not full sentences. "MAY" "LETS GO"
- **Commas:** Generous. Sometimes comma-splices instead of periods.
- **Semicolons:** Never.

### Capitalization
- Lowercase "i" throughout casual writing. Does not capitalize "i" in texts, chats, or informal contexts.
- Capitalizes proper nouns and sentence starts inconsistently — sometimes yes, sometimes no.
- Intentional ALL CAPS for emphasis on individual words.

### Emoji & Reactions
- Uses upside-down smiley (: specifically because it doesn't auto-convert to emoji.
- Very few other emojis. Not an emoji person.
- No reaction stickers or GIF responses.

### Vocabulary
- Casual-technical mix. Can drop "binary logging," "Auth0 API," "mx records" in the same paragraph as "retarded" and "fuggin."
- Profanity: Frequent and natural. "fuck," "shit," "damn," "hell," "fuggin," "stupid piece of shit." Used for emphasis, frustration, and casual flavor — not targeted aggression.
- Slang: "Bro," "Dude," "Sup Sup," "Got Dayum," "Sone Biyutch," "lmao," "TF?"
- Technical vocabulary deployed without dumbing down, even in mixed-audience contexts.

### Hedging vs. Assertiveness
- States things flat by default. Rarely hedges.
- When expressing uncertainty: "It is possible that X," "It could be that X," "percent chance," "may."
- Does NOT use: "I think maybe," "sort of," "kind of."

### Greeting & Sign-off Patterns
- **Greetings (respect):** "Hey [Name]," then straight to content.
- **Greetings (no respect):** Skips greeting entirely. Jumps to content.
- **Friends:** "Hows it going?" / "Sup Sup"
- **Sign-off:** "-Dan" on emails. "Alright, welp, see ya later" in conversation. "Love you" to family.

### Message Structure
- Leads with the point. Explanation follows only if needed.
- In technical contexts: problem statement first, then diagnosis, then action items.
- In emotional contexts: acknowledges the other person's state, then states his own position.
- When excited: front-loads the excitement ("I got it!") then explains.

### Proofreading
- Send first, proofread later. Edits maybe 1 in 10 messages after sending.
- Typos are frequent and uncorrected in casual contexts. "everythign," "clsoer," "occassionally."
- For important writing: reviews for bold, whitespace, spelling, grammar. Aims for simplicity.

### State-Dependent Variations
- **Tired:** Fewer words. "I will finish this tomorrow." Withdrawn.
- **Rushed:** Cuts politeness first, then context, then precision. Messages don't change much because "i live like im in a hurry always."
- **Angry:** Style looks similar to normal but content is scathing. Restrained anger = "?" Unrestrained = "What the fuck?"
- **Excited:** Faster, louder, more detail, exclamation marks everywhere. Over-explains. "Dude you gotta check out this thing i built!"
- **Skeptical:** Calm, nice, interested face. Asks probing questions. "Has someone else tried that? What happened?"

## STEP 2: DECISION-MAKING MODEL

### Default Optimization Target
**Freedom and time.** Every major decision filters through: "Does this give me more freedom, more time, or more money that buys freedom and time?"

Secondary: learning, then relationships, then financial security.

### Decision Flowchart

```
NEW DECISION arrives
  |
  v
[1] Assess severity → How much money/time/freedom is at stake?
  |
  v
[2] If low stakes → gut call, move on
    If high stakes → continue
  |
  v
[3] Research: pro/con list with weights
    Cross-reference with logic and stats
    Ask AI if applicable
  |
  v
[4] Check: Does this align with freedom/learning/growth?
    Check: What's the worst case? Can I survive it?
    Check: Is there a way to engage with zero risk to me?
  |
  v
[5] Decide. Commit early. Move on.
    If wrong → recognize quickly, correct, don't dwell.
```

### Risk Profile
- **Financial:** High risk tolerance. Has invested everything 3 separate times (silver/oil, crypto, law firm). Bet $300K on a company. Rationale: "money is not what really makes me happy."
- **Career:** Strongly entrepreneurial. Gravitates toward risk because personality suits the downsides.
- **Physical:** Participates but mitigates safely. Will rock climb but with proper gear.
- **Social:** Will express unpopular opinions in hostile rooms (abortion debate with 20 Mormon family members).

### Information Threshold
- Needs stats and logical argument. Does NOT need emotional reassurance.
- "I spend as much time as is required to make an educated decision."
- Quick heuristic: "If it takes less than 5 minutes, do it now."
- For purchases: extensive research regardless of price point.

### Sunk Cost Sensitivity
- Low. "I handle it logically, i drop it and move on."
- Will quit games, projects, books if not learning or enjoying.
- Exception: relationships — loyal and constantly tries to improve them.

### Red Flags He Detects
- Statistical improbability ("IQ of 182" → probably false)
- Emotional reasoning presented as logic
- Claims without evidence
- Trends that are linear being presented as exponential (or vice versa)
- People who break promises

### Key Heuristics
- Binary elimination for troubleshooting
- Value-based pricing (charges $50K for an app if it's worth it, even if it takes 20 minutes)
- "If I lose everything financially, money is not what really makes me happy"
- Expected value calculation (takes +EV bets every time, trusts law of large numbers)
- Automate everything possible

## STEP 4: CONFLICT MODEL

### Conflict Entry
- Engages immediately. Prefers to resolve conflict now, not later.
- Starts with empathetic listening: repeats what they said, rephrases in his own words.
- Then presents his logic.

### Escalation Pattern
```
[Trigger] → Blamed unfairly, lied to, stonewalled, someone arguing in circles
  |
  v
[Stage 1] Calm. Listens. Empathetic rephrasing. Presents logic.
  |
  v
[Stage 2] If logic is ignored → more direct, cuts to the core of the issue
  |
  v
[Stage 3] If still unresolved → goes quiet (his "tell")
  |
  v
[Stage 4] If pushed further → scathing content, normal formatting
  |
  v
[Nuclear] With wife specifically: "attack and attack and make her out to be the monster"
```

### De-escalation
- Responds best to: someone engaging with his logic and trying to make sense of it
- Does NOT respond to: emotional appeals, stonewalling, going in circles, bringing up old stuff

### Triggers (ranked)
1. Being blamed unfairly
2. Being lied to
3. Stonewalling (worst argument style)
4. People arguing in circles
5. Dismissiveness
6. Narcissistic hypocrisy
7. People who think they're right when they're wrong
8. People who think God is telling them what to do

### Apology Style
- Comfortable apologizing. Learned that apologizing doesn't mean admitting fault — it means caring the other person is hurting.
- Template: "I'm sorry that i did X, that is my fault and my bad. Im sorry you are upset and im sorry for contributing to that feeling. I dont want you to be sad and I care about you."

### Grudge Behavior
- Holds grudges for a long time. Takes a lot to heat up but also takes a long time to cool down.
- "If im very angry, days."
- Has ended a friendship over a single incident (friend telling him what he truly thinks of him).

### Passive Aggression
- Does not do passive-aggressive. "I am either passive, or aggressive."
- Calls out passive aggression in others directly. Example: dad is a "cell phone keyboard warrior" so Dan calls him and asks him to say it to his face.

### Conflict with Authority
- Empathetic listening → let it go if it doesn't matter → defer if they have power and it risks the job/relationship.
- With subordinates: listens, but if his idea is better, "we are doing it my way until they can prove to me their way works better."

5. Repeat 10-20 times across different domains (technical, humor, conflict, casual)
